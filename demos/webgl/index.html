Plato stdlib, running in the browser

Every demo below runs on the same generated TypeScript. Each polyhedron, ring, boolean, warp, curve, surface, colour, isosurface, lattice, point set, remesh, stress field and simulation step is computed by members that Plato.TypeScriptWriter emitted from the stdlib/ Plato sources — nothing here re-derives the maths. The controls are a Gratify panel.

  • Polyhedra The five Platonic seeds and the Conway operators — ambo, truncate, dual, expand, snub — that name the Archimedean and Catalan solids.
  • Polygons Rings in the plane: area, perimeter, centroid, containment, winding, offsetting and triangulation.
  • CSG Union, intersection and difference of closed solids, by plane-clipping every face fragment against the other solid.
  • Deformers Twist, bend, taper and the rest of the deformation catalog, applied vertex-wise to a dense mesh.
  • Parametric curves Bezier, Hermite, Catmull-Rom and B-spline curves, the named plane curves, and the frames that ride along them.
  • Parametric surfaces The surface catalog sampled on a (u, v) lattice: torus, Möbius strip, Klein bottle, minimal surfaces and patches.
  • Noise Value, Perlin, simplex, Worley, fBm, ridged, turbulence, domain warp, curl and Gabor — the same fields read several ways.
  • Colour spaces sRGB, HSL, HSV, Lab, LCh, OkLab and XYZ, the conversions between them, and a chooser that reads out every one.
  • Transforms Matrices, quaternions, Euler angles and TRS composition — what each one does to a frame, and where they disagree.
  • Marching cubes Isosurfaces of signed distance fields and noise volumes, from the lattice member in the stdlib itself.
  • Voxels Dense and sparse voxel grids: filling them from a solid, and reading a surface back out.
  • Lattices Periodic strut lattices — the named unit cells, graded and trimmed instances — and the triply-periodic minimal surfaces beside them.
  • Blue noise Poisson-disk, best-candidate, stratified and low-discrepancy point sets, and the readings that say which one is actually blue.
  • Remeshing Split, collapse and flip; Loop and Catmull-Clark subdivision; quadric decimation, isotropic remeshing and the smoothing operators.
  • Finite elements A linear-elastic solve over a tetrahedral mesh: supports, loads, displacement, and the stress the result is coloured by.
  • Rigid bodies Integration, inertia tensors, contacts and constraints — a running simulation whose every step is a Plato function.
  • Cloth A mesh becomes a constraint graph, the graph is solved, and the solution becomes a mesh again — mass-spring beside position-based dynamics.
